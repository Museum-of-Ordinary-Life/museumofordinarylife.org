Field Notes

Ways of noticing

Field note / Photography

Photograph the object twice.

First as an object. Then where it actually, ordinarily lives. A key on a blank surface tells us what a key looked like. A key beside the door, receipts and yesterday’s mail tells us about a life.

Field note / Digital life

Interfaces disappear quickly.

Screenshots of mundane software, payment prompts, settings pages and error messages may become harder to reconstruct than physical objects. Capture them before their inevitable redesign or phase-out.

Field note / Meaning

Nothing profound is required.

An object doesn't need a moving story to belong at the museum. “This is the mug I use every morning” is historical information. As is “I don’t remember why I kept this.”

Archive ethics / Data & AI

A record of humanity should not become a free-for-all dataset.

A sufficiently large archive of ordinary life is also, inevitably, a dataset about humanity: how people actually lived, what they kept, what they ignored, how their homes and streets worked, what their software looked like, what they worried about, and what an ordinary Tuesday felt like.

That makes the collection potentially useful to historians, researchers, artists and computational systems. It also makes consent part of the archival record itself. The Museum therefore treats public display, research, machine learning and generative reuse as different uses rather than one blanket permission.

Preservation and publication are different decisions too. A contribution can enter the archive now without becoming public now: a contributor may choose immediate display, anonymous display, release on or after a particular date, a delay of a chosen number of years, a request to be asked again later, or archival preservation without public display.

MOL / DISPLAY

Public display

May this contribution appear publicly in the Museum, an exhibition, publication or public archive? If yes, it does not have to appear immediately.

MOL / EMBARGO

Delayed release

Publish now, publish on or after a chosen date, wait a chosen number of years, ask again later, or preserve it without public display. The archive can keep something before the world is ready to see it.

MOL / RESEARCH

Research

May researchers study the contribution as part of historical, cultural, social or technical research?

MOL / LEARN

Machine learning

May computational systems analyze or learn patterns from the contribution without treating generative reuse as automatically permitted?

MOL / GENERATE

Generative reuse

May a system reproduce, remix, imitate or generate material from the contribution? This is a separate permission from machine learning.

MOL / CREDIT

Identity & attribution

Should reuse identify the contributor, remain anonymous, or preserve attribution only inside the archive?

MOL / FUTURE

Future uses

For uses that do not yet exist or cannot yet be meaningfully described: allow, decline, or ask again.

MOL / WITHDRAW

Change your mind

Contributors should be able to revise permissions or request withdrawal where technically and legally feasible.
