Communities anywhere may create their own collections, exhibitions and museum structures—connected to the wider Museum without becoming subordinate to it. The Museum provides shared connective infrastructure; communities decide what ordinary life means where they are, how it should be described, and what should remain local, restricted, delayed or offline.

01

Shared globally

Preservation, backups, interoperable cataloging, consent and embargo systems, translation, accessibility, documentation and a discovery layer connecting independent collections.

02

Shaped locally

Each community chooses what it collects, which languages it uses, how it interprets material, where physical objects remain, and how stewardship is shared or rotated.

03

Connected by choice

A local museum can stand on its own, collaborate on an exhibition, or make selected records discoverable across the network without surrendering ownership, context or local authority.

It does not need a building

A museum can be a lasting community institution—or exist for a day, a season, one question or one exhibition. Its form should follow the lives and stories it is trying to hold.

01

Rooted somewhere

An ongoing neighborhood, village, city or regional collection, cared for by people who know the place from within.

02

Here for a while

A pop-up museum, a year-long archive, a single public gathering or an exhibition assembled for one particular moment.

03

Made to move

A traveling collection, a table at a market, a room inside another community space, or a museum carried from place to place.

04

Distributed

A digital, diasporic, language-based or thematic museum whose community is connected by experience rather than one location.

A home within the network

Participating museums could have a recognizable home within the wider Museum while keeping their own identity, languages, collection, exhibitions and stewardship.

sf.museumofordinarylife.orgkumasi.museumofordinarylife.orgdhaka.museumofordinarylife.orgmekongdelta.museumofordinarylife.orgqueerdomestic.museumofordinarylife.org

A Museum subdomain would be a provided home, not a requirement. A community could use its own domain, keep a collection offline, or connect only selected material. Several overlapping museums could also coexist in the same place; none would have to become the single authoritative account of that community.

What belongs in a museum of ordinary life?

Almost anything, provided it helps explain how everyday life actually worked. The Museum values ubiquity, context and lived experience (at least as much as rarity).
